How to make a Christmas escape room at home

By Paul, creator of Christmas Escape Room · Updated 22 November 2026

To make a Christmas escape room at home, pick a festive mission, work backwards from where the prize is hidden, and write 5–7 clues whose answers each name the next hiding place. It takes 1–2 hours to plan and about 10 minutes to hide the clues.

Time to make1–2 hours
Set-up on the day10–15 minutes
Play time30–60 minutes
Puzzles5–7 (4 for under-7s)
You needPaper or card, a pen, envelopes, a small prize. Padlocks optional.

Step by step

  1. Pick a story and a goal. Choose a simple Christmas mission, such as ‘find Santa’s lost key before midnight’ or ‘rescue the stolen pudding’. The goal tells players what the final answer is.
  2. Work backwards from the ending. Decide where the final prize is hidden, then where the clue before it is hidden, and so on. Each answer should name the next hiding place.
  3. Choose five to seven puzzle types. Mix them up: a riddle, a word scramble, a number code (A=1), a backwards message, a cipher, a maths lock and a picture search.
  4. Make the clues. Write each clue on a card. Use our Christmas cipher maker for pigpen and Caesar codes, and print a copy of the answers for yourself.
  5. Test it on one person. Ask someone who isn’t playing to solve each card. If it takes longer than 10 minutes, make it easier or add a hint.
  6. Hide the clues and set the scene. Hide the cards in order, dim the lights, put on Christmas music and start a countdown timer on the TV.
  7. Run the game. Read the story aloud, hand over the first card and start the clock. Give a hint when a team is stuck for 3–5 minutes.
  8. Celebrate the finish. Reveal the prize, announce the time and hand out certificates.

Christmas escape room puzzle ideas

  1. Riddle: “I have needles but never sew” → the Christmas tree.
  2. Word scramble: GNIKCOTS → STOCKING.
  3. Number code (A=1, B=2): 19-15-6-1 → SOFA.
  4. Caesar shift: move each letter back three: FKLPQHB → CHIMNEY.
  5. Pigpen cipher: make one with our cipher maker.
  6. Backwards message: DRAOBPUC → CUPBOARD.
  7. Acrostic poem: the first letter of each line spells the answer.
  8. Maths lock: reindeer (9) × days of Christmas (12) + Christmas Day (25) = 133.
  9. Count the baubles: the number of red baubles on the tree is the code.
  10. Cracker joke: the punchline’s first letters spell the next place.

Make it easier or harder

  • Ages 4–6: use picture clues, read every card aloud and hide clues in plain sight.
  • Teenagers and adults: add a 30-minute limit, a red herring or two, and a combination padlock on the final box.
  • Big groups: make two copies of every clue and run two teams side by side.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to make a Christmas escape room?

About 1–2 hours to plan and write five puzzles, and 10–15 minutes to hide the clues on the day. Using a ready-made printable cuts this to 10 minutes.

How many puzzles should a home escape room have?

Five to seven puzzles suits most families and fills 30–60 minutes. Use four for children under 7 and up to ten for experienced adult teams.

Do I need padlocks for a home escape room?

No. Each answer can simply name the next hiding place. If you want locks, a cheap 3- or 4-digit combination padlock on a box or bag adds drama.

What are good hiding places for Christmas escape room clues?

Inside a stocking, under the Christmas tree, in a cracker box, under a sofa cushion, in the fridge door, inside a book or behind a picture frame.

How do I stop players getting stuck?

Give each team three hint tokens, keep an answer sheet, and test every puzzle on one person before the game.
