Christmas Memory Lane: a seated escape room for care homes and older adults

By Paul, creator of Christmas Escape Room · Updated 22 November 2026

Christmas Memory Lane is a free large-print Christmas escape room for care home residents and older adults. Played seated around a table, a host reads gentle festive puzzles aloud, and each answer earns a letter or number to open the ‘Christmas box’. It takes 20–30 minutes for groups of 3–10.

Who forCare home residents, day centres, older relatives at home
Players3–10, seated
Time20–30 minutes
Set-up5 minutes

If nothing happened, press Ctrl + P (Windows) or ⌘ + P (Mac). Each card prints on its own page; host notes don’t print.

The story

Father Christmas has locked his box of treats and lost the code. Residents help him by remembering Christmases past. Every round earns part of the code word, and the box opens when the word is complete. The rounds are designed to start conversations as much as to be solved.

What’s in the game

  1. Round 1: Christmas memories: five gentle festive questions; first letters spell a word.
  2. Round 2: Sing it out: a group sing-along.
  3. Round 3: The Twelve Days: counting with the Twelve Days of Christmas.

How to set it up

  1. Print the cards below. Each card prints on its own sheet and the host notes are left off.
  2. Hide each card in the place shown in its host note and in the answer key. Start from the last card and work backwards.
  3. Put the treats and the final card in a box or tin in the middle of the table.
  4. Start the escape room timer and hand Card 1 to the players.

Make it easier or harder

  • Easier: give multiple-choice options for each question (for example, “Cracker, candle or card?”).
  • Harder: add your own local memories, such as the town’s Christmas lights or a well-known shop window display.

Frequently asked questions

Can you do an escape room in a care home?

Yes. Christmas Memory Lane is designed for care homes: it’s seated, large print and read aloud by one host, with gentle questions that bring back Christmas memories. It takes 20–30 minutes.

Is it suitable for residents with dementia?

Many groups with mild memory loss enjoy it because the questions are about long-held Christmas traditions. Go at the group’s pace, give plenty of prompts and celebrate every answer. Activity staff know their residents best.

How many people can play?

3–10 residents around one table works best. For larger lounges, split into two tables with a staff member each.

What do I need?

A printer, a box or tin, and a treat such as mince pies. Print the cards in large print; they are already set at a bigger size.
