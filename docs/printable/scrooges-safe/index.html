Scrooge’s Safe: free printable Christmas escape room for adults

By Paul, creator of Christmas Escape Room · Updated 22 November 2026

Scrooge’s Safe is a free printable Christmas escape room for adults and teenagers 14+. Based on A Christmas Carol, players crack a Caesar shift, a pigpen cipher, mirror writing and a four-digit logic lock to open Scrooge’s safe. It takes 45–60 minutes for 2–6 players.

Answer key

If nothing happened, press Ctrl + P (Windows) or ⌘ + P (Mac). Each card prints on its own page; host notes don’t print.

The story

It’s Christmas Eve, 1843. Ebenezer Scrooge has locked his fortune in a safe and gone to bed grumbling. Players must follow the clues left by the ghosts who will visit him that night, open the safe and give the money to the Cratchits before Christmas morning.

What’s in the game

  1. A note from Marley: a Caesar shift where players must work out the shift from the story.
  2. The Ghost of Christmas Past: a pigpen cipher with the key included.
  3. The Ghost of Christmas Present: mirror writing.
  4. The Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come: a logic lock that needs knowledge of A Christmas Carol.

How to set it up

  1. Print the cards below. Each card prints on its own sheet and the host notes are left off.
  2. Hide each card in the place shown in its host note and in the answer key. Start from the last card and work backwards.
  3. Put the final card and a prize in a box or bag. Lock it with a 4-digit padlock set to 9371, or have the host check the code.
  4. Start the escape room timer and hand Card 1 to the players.

Make it easier or harder

  • Easier: tell players there are four ghosts, and let them use the full pigpen key from the start.
  • Harder: remove the pigpen key from card 2 and hide it somewhere else in the room, and set a 40-minute limit.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free printable Christmas escape room for adults?

Yes. Scrooge’s Safe is free, designed for adults and teens 14+, and takes 45–60 minutes. It uses a pigpen cipher, mirror writing, a Caesar shift and a logic lock based on A Christmas Carol.

Do I need to know A Christmas Carol?

It helps for the final lock, but the story section gives players enough to work it out: Marley visits first, then the ghosts of Christmas Past, Present and Yet to Come.

Do I need a padlock?

No. A 4-digit padlock set to 9371 adds drama, but the host can simply check the code and hand over the final card.

Can we play it at a Christmas dinner party?

Yes. Set it up before guests arrive and play between courses. Teams of 2–3 work best; make two copies to race.

How hard is it?

Harder than our family games. Most adult teams finish in 45–60 minutes with one or two hints.
