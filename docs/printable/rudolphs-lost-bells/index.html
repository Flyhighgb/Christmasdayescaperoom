Rudolph’s Lost Bells: Christmas escape room for toddlers and preschoolers

By Paul, creator of Christmas Escape Room · Updated 22 November 2026

Rudolph’s Lost Bells is a free picture-based Christmas escape room for children aged 3–5. No reading is needed: each card shows a big picture of where to look next, with simple counting and colour games. It takes 15–20 minutes, with a grown-up reading the story.

Ages3–5 (and 6-year-olds who are new to escape rooms)
Players1–4 children with a grown-up
Time15–20 minutes
Set-up5 minutes
DifficultyVery easy
Reading neededNone. Picture clues only.

The story

Oh no! Rudolph has lost the jingle bells from his harness and can’t fly on Christmas Eve without them. Little helpers follow Rudolph’s picture clues around the house to find the bells. A grown-up reads each card aloud.

What’s in the game

  1. Rudolph needs help!: a picture clue.
  2. Count with Rudolph: a counting game, then a picture clue.
  3. Colour hunt: a colour hunt, then a picture clue.
  4. Rudolph’s snack: a matching clue.

How to set it up

  1. Print the cards below. Each card prints on its own sheet and the host notes are left off.
  2. Hide each card in the place shown in its host note and in the answer key. Start from the last card and work backwards.
  3. Put the final card with a bell or small treat in the kitchen.
  4. Start the escape room timer and hand Card 1 to the players.

Make it easier or harder

  • Easier: leave each card in plain sight and walk with the children to each spot.
  • Harder: for 5–6-year-olds, hide the cards properly and add the Great Pudding Heist straight afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

Can toddlers do an escape room?

Yes, if it uses pictures instead of words. Rudolph’s Lost Bells is built for ages 3–5: a grown-up reads each card and the children match the picture to a place in the house.

How long does it take?

About 15–20 minutes, which suits young attention spans. Set-up takes 5 minutes.

Can older siblings help?

Yes. Older children can be ‘Rudolph’s helpers’ and read the cards aloud to the little ones.

What should the prize be?

A jingle bell, a sticker or a small treat works well. Little ones love ringing a bell at the end.
